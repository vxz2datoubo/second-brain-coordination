# Calibration and Identification Plan

Identification is limited: an observed trace can be compatible with several participant hypotheses. The engine must distinguish predictive association, a simulated best response and causal attribution. It cannot use future data, later labels, post-hoc news or a source's later explanation to justify an earlier decision state.

Before any empirical study, freeze the rule and security-status versions, source manifests, availability timestamps, target variable, hypothesis space, priors, action feasibility, cost model, split scheme and scoring rules. Evaluate posterior calibration with Brier score and log loss where numerical probabilities are available, and retain abstentions and unresolved cases. Use placebo windows, negative controls, alternative-label tests, regime stratification and sensitivity analysis to test whether results survive competing explanations.

No calibration gate can promote a participant hypothesis into a real identity, a strategy into a tradable system, or a model into an execution path.
